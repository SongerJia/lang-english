### Group 15

Walk along the streets of Beijing and listen carefully to the little babies calling for their mothers and what do you hear? Mama. On the other side of the world, you’ll hear Spanish children crying for mama while Italian and French babies want their mamma and maman respectively. How did one syllable become so universal in children’s vocabularies over the world?

Some experts believe that the simplest explanation lies in the very first sounds babies make. Hungry babies only have to open their mouths wide and scream AAAAAAH to pronounce their first vowel—A. Later, as they start playing around with their mouths, all they have to do is to close and open their lips to make an M, their very first consonant. That is to say, they’ve discovered another sound! Adults hearing babies making this charming string of mamama sounds might think that the babies are calling out to them. A mother would then naturally assume that she’s the one being addressed and so would refer to herself as mama!
