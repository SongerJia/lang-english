### Group 1

Many people associate their self-worth with their work. The more successful their career, the better they feel about themselves. Work-related self-esteem is therefore a worthy ideal to pursue with vigour, right? Well, not always.

It’s natural to be drawn towards pleasure and to step away from pain. In the workplace, if that pleasure comes from a triumph which swells our self-respect, people will try to repeat the accomplishment. But repeating that accomplishment is often not realistic, which can lead to severe negative emotional consequences when it doesn’t reoccur. This form of motivation is widely regarded as a negative type of motivation. It can hinder other more positive motivation types, such as completing a task purely because it’s fulfilling or enjoyable.

Studies discovered several positive outcomes that can actually outweigh the harmful ones. Though these types of employees are motivated by the desire to avoid negative consequences, they are also motivated by the excitement of pursuing emotional rewards. This excitement makes pursuing goals enjoyable and stimulates pleasure and pride that would result from success.

### Group 2

Trust is fundamental to life. If you can’t trust anything, life becomes intolerable. You can’t have relationships without trust, let alone good ones.

In the workplace, too, trust is essential. An organization without trust will be full of fear and suspicion. If you work for a boss who doesn’t trust their employees to do things right, you’ll have a miserable time. They’ll be checking up on you all the time, correcting “mistakes” and constantly reminding you to do this or that.

Think of all the additional tasks caused by lack of trust. Audit departments only exist because of it. Companies keep large volumes of records because they don’t trust their suppliers, their contractors or their customers. Probably more than half of all administrative work is only there because of an ever-existing sense that “you can’t trust anyone these days”.

All this is extra work we load onto ourselves because we don’t trust people—the checking, following through, doing things ourselves because we don’t believe others will do them properly—or at all. If we took all that away, how much extra time would we suddenly find in our life? How much of our work pressure would disappear?

### Group 3

The work-life balance is dead. By this, I’m not advocating that you should give up your pursuit of having a fulfilling career and a thriving personal life. I acknowledge that we have a work-life problem, but I’m arguing that the concept of balance has never been helpful, because it’s too limiting.

In the ideal world, we’re all doing work that we’re proud of and that provides meaning and purpose to us. But even if your job doesn’t give you shivers of joy each new day, working is a part of what each of us does and the contribution we make to society. When you separate work and life, it’s a little bit harder to make that connection.

A goal to balance suggests that things could quickly get off balance, and that causes terrible outcomes. It’s more constructive to think of solutions that continue to evolve over shifts in life and work. These variations are normal, and it’s more useful to think of life as something that is ever evolving and changing, rather than high-risk enterprise where things could go wrong with one misstep.

Let’s bury “work-life balance” and think bigger and better about work-life fulfillment to do a little less balancing and a lot more living.

### Group 4

悉尼大学的研究人员在《社交网络》上发表了一篇论文，报告了参与者对团建干预措施（intervention）的感受，揭示了强迫员工参加团建所涉及的道德问题。

该论文的首席研究员彼得·马图斯博士说：“许多人认为团建活动是浪费时间，因此我们决定更深入地研究其背后的原因。”

研究发现，团建活动的重点是分享和干预（intervene）团队成员之间的个人态度与关系，这些活动被认为过于粗暴（heavy-handed）并具有侵犯性（intrusive），尽管研究人员说，要与同事建立深层次的有效联系，一定程度的坦诚（openness）和脆弱（vulnerability）往往是必要的。

马托斯说：“一些参与者反对团建活动，因为这些活动隐含（implicitly）着强制性。他们不欢迎管理层在他们的直接工作表现之外关注他们的生活，许多人不愿意被迫（force）去找乐子或交朋友，尤其是在繁忙的工作之余。他们觉得管理层太爱管闲事（nosy），或者试图过多地控制他们的生活。”

### Group 5

Workers at Mexican oil company will receive a healthy incentive of almost $ 300 a year if they meet certain body weight standards. To qualify for the bonus, they must maintain a healthy weight. For those who are overweight or obese, they could receive the bonus if they reduce their weight by 10% during the year.

Some applaud the policy as fair, because it rewards both the individuals who maintain a healthy weight and those who are working towards achieving it.

But critics say the policy contradicts recommendations by many health experts, who warn that a person’s weight is influenced by genetic and environmental factors. That means weight can be hard for individuals to control, which makes the bonus unfair.

Some experts say the policy is inconsistent with the science of what we know about body weight regulation. A more productive method of encouraging health among employees is to promote healthy behaviours. For example, companies can give workers gym memberships. They can also reduce sweets and provide healthier snacks in the office and healthier meals at the company canteen. These are much better and much more productive ways for companies to move forward on their employees’ well-being.

短文翻译

墨西哥石油公司的工人如果达到一定的体重标准，每年将获得近300美元的健康激励（incentive）金。要想有资格获得该奖金（bonus），他们必须保持健康的体重。对于那些超重或肥胖的工人，如果他们在一年中将体重降低10%，就可以获得奖金。

一些人称赞（applaud）这项政策是公平的（fair），因为它既奖励（reward）保持健康体重的人，也奖励正在努力实现健康体重的人。

但批评者（critic）认为，这项政策与许多健康专家的建议相悖（contradict），这些健康专家警告说，一个人的体重受遗传（genetic）和环境因素的影响。这意味着个人很难控制体重，这就使得奖金奖励并不公平（unfair）。

一些专家说，这项政策不符合（inconsistent）我们对体重管理（regulation）的科学认识。促进员工身体健康的更有效方法是鼓励健康行为。例如，公司可以为员工提供健身房会员资格（membership）。公司还可以在办公室减少甜食供应，提供更健康的零食（snack），在公司食堂（canteen）提供更健康的膳食。这些都是公司促进员工健康的更好、更有成效的方法。
