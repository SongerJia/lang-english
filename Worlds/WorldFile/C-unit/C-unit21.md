### Group 2

Archaeologists in Pompeii, the city buried in a volcanic eruption in 79 AD, have made the extraordinary find of a frescoed hot food and drinks shop that served up the ancient equivalent of street food to Roman passers-by.

Traces of nearly 2,000-year-old food were found in some of the deep terra cotta jars containing hot food which the shop keeper lowered into a counter with circular holes.

The front of the counter was decorated with brightly coloured frescoes, some depicting animals that were part of the ingredients in the food sold, such as a chicken and two ducks hanging upside down.

Archaeologists also found a decorated bronze drinking bowl known as a patera, ceramic jars used for cooking stews and soups, wine flasks and amphorae.

“Our preliminary analyses show that the figures drawn on the front of the counter, represent, at least in part, the food and drink that were sold there,” said Valeria Amoretti, a site anthropologist.

Amoretti said traces of pork, fish, snails and beef had been found in the containers, a discovery she called a “testimony to the great variety of animal products used to prepare dishes.”

在一些装有热食的深陶（terra cotta）罐中发现了近2000年前食物的痕迹（trace），店员将这些陶罐放进一个有圆（circular）孔的柜台中。

### Group 3

The city of Bath was founded by the Romans almost two thousand years ago. It has been famous for its aesthetically pleasing architecture and healing thermal springs ever since.

There are three hot springs in Bath; one is the King’s Spring, upon which the Roman Baths and a temple were constructed. The other two are the Cross Bath and the Hetling Spring.

While the Georgians made “taking the waters” or bathing particularly fashionable, it was previous generations who paved the way, creating greater interest in Bath and its springs. Charles II, desperate for an heir and unable to produce a legitimate son, came to Bath to take the waters in the hope that their magical powers would do something to remedy the situation. Craving for a male heir, James and Mary both came to Bath and soon after produced a son, which bred many conspiracy theories about who was the real father of their offspring. Regardless, the “miracle” created something of a boom in tourism for Bath.

Afterwards, the spas in Bath continued to go in and out of fashion for more than 150 years until they closed completely. The new Bath Spa, which opened in 2006, incorporates modern architecture with the ancient spring, now the New Royal Bath.

尽管是乔治王朝时期的人让“泡温泉”或沐浴变得盛行（fashionable），但是前几代人为此创造了条件，让人们对巴斯及其温泉产生了更大的兴趣。当时，查理二世急需（desperate）一位继承人（heir），却没能生下合法婚生（legitimate）子，于是他来到巴斯的温泉沐浴，希望（in the hope that）温泉的魔力能挽救（remedy）局面。由于迫切需要（crave）一位男性继承人，詹姆斯和玛丽来到了巴斯，不久之后就生下（produce）了一个儿子，这引发了许多关于谁才是这个孩子的亲生父亲的阴谋（conspiracy）论。无论如何，这个“奇迹”在某种程度上为巴斯的旅游业带来了繁荣（boom）。

### Group 4

The Caribbean islands are divided into two worlds, a rich one and a poor one. This tropical region’s economy is based mainly on farming. Farmers are of two types. One is the plantation owner who may have hundreds of thousands of acres. In contrast, the small cultivator is working only a few acres of land. Most visitors to the Caribbean are rich, like the plantation owner.

The Caribbean produces many things. Sugar is the main product. Other export crops are tobacco, coffee, bananas, spices and citrus fruits, such as orange, lemon or grapefruit. From the West Indies also come oil, mineral pitch and many forest products. Jamaica’s aluminium ore supplies are the world’s largest. Oil comes from Trinidad, Aruba and Curacao. But for many of the smaller islands, sugar is the only export. Rum, a strong alcoholic drink—which is distilled from sugar cane—is also an export. The world’s best rum comes from this area.

The Caribbean islands have been favourite places to visit. The islanders have built elaborate resorts, developed harbours and airfields, improved beaches and have expanded sea and air routes. Everything is at the resort—hotel, beach, shopping and recreation. The vacationer never has any reason to explore the island.

加勒比群岛被分成了两个世界，一个富裕的世界和一个贫穷的世界。这个热带地区的经济（economy）主要以农业（farming）为基础。农民分为两类。一类是种植园主（plantation owner），他们可能拥有数十万英亩（acre）的土地。相比之下，另一类小种植户（cultivator）只耕种几英亩的土地。大多数去加勒比海的游客都很富有，就像种植园主一样。

加勒比海地区出产许多东西。蔗糖是主要产品。其他出口（export）作物有烟草（tobacco）、咖啡、香蕉、香料和柑橘类（citrus）水果，如橙子、柠檬或葡萄柚（grapefruit）。西印度群岛还出产石油、矿物沥青和许多林产品。牙买加的铝（aluminium）矿石（ore）供应量居世界首位。特立尼达、阿鲁巴和库拉索岛出产石油。但对于许多较小的岛屿来说，蔗糖是唯一的出口产品。朗姆酒（rum），一种烈性酒类（alcoholic）饮品——从甘蔗（sugar cane）中蒸馏（distil）出来的——也是一种出口产品。世界上最好的朗姆酒就产自这一地区。

加勒比群岛一直是人们最喜欢去的地方。岛民（islander）建造了精致的度假村（resort），开发了（develop）港口（harbour）和机场（airfield），改善了海滩环境，并扩大了海运和空运航线（route）。度假村应有尽有——酒店、海滩、购物和娱乐。度假者（vacationer）完全不需要去岛的其他地方。

### Group 5

Throwing spare change into a fountain is a time-honoured ritual: throw a penny into the water, and your wish might come true. But all that money has to go somewhere. Otherwise, the growing piles of pennies, quarters and euros could clog up the fountain’s works.

Well, the coins collected can go to all sorts of different places—from fountain maintenance to charity or public service. In New York City, for example, coins collected from fountains in public parks often go towards the fountains’ maintenance itself, though entrepreneurs who don’t mind getting their hands wet often get to it first. The fountains in New York City parks are cleaned by the park’s staff every few weeks, but most of the coins have already been removed by entrepreneurial New Yorkers, and there is not a significant amount left to be collected.

Rome’s famous Trevi fountain: for hundreds of years, visitors have thrown coins over their shoulder into the fountain to ensure that they will return. So many tourists toss in coins that Roman officials have the fountain cleaned every night, reportedly getting as much as $4,000 in loose change from around the world each day.

For the most part, money collected from privately-owned fountains in the United States also goes to charity. Whether or not your wish comes true after tossing a coin into a fountain, you can rest assured knowing that the change is likely going to someone who needs it.

短文翻译

把不用的硬币扔到喷泉（fountain）里是一种历史悠久的（time-honoured）仪式（ritual）：往水里扔一分钱，你的愿望就可能实现。但所有那些钱都得有去处。否则（otherwise），便士（penny）、美分（quarter）和欧元硬币越堆（pile）越多，可能会堵塞（clog）喷泉的部件（works）。

其实，收集到的硬币可以用于各种不同的地方——从喷泉维护（maintenance）到慈善或公共服务。例如，在纽约市，从公园喷泉收集的硬币通常会用于（go towards）喷泉本身的维护，不过那些不介意弄湿双手的“创业者们”（entrepreneur）通常抢先一步对硬币下手。纽约城市公园喷泉的工作人员每隔几周会对喷泉进行清理，但是大多数硬币都已经被那些“富有创业精神的”纽约人拿走了，没剩下多少可以收集的了。

罗马著名的特雷维喷泉：数百年来，游客们将硬币高高抛起扔进喷泉，以确保他们还会再来。由于扔（toss）硬币的游客太多，罗马公职人员每晚都要清理喷泉，据说（reportedly）每天收到来自世界各地的零（loose）钱多达4000美元。

大多数情况下，从美国私营（privately-owned）喷泉收集的资金也用于慈善事业。把硬币扔进喷泉后，无论你的愿望是否实现，你都可以放心（assured），因为你的零钱很可能会送到有需要的人手中。
