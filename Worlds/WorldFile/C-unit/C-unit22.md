### Group 1

More than 20 percent of the world’s population relies on chopsticks for eating. So how did two sticks start a massive mealtime revolution?

China was the first to experiment with chopsticks nearly 3,000 years ago. But at that time, they weren’t used as eating utensils. The Chinese used them for cooking since they could safely dip them into boiling pots of water.

Chopsticks are different throughout Asian cultures. Chinese chopsticks, for instance, are long and thick “to facilitate dining around the table”.

In Japan, where bamboo chopsticks were adopted in 500 C.E. for religious ceremonies, chopsticks have evolved drastically over time. They’re now particularly fine-tuned for one of Japan’s main foods: fish. Japanese chopsticks are short and sharp, mainly because they are good at eating fish, and it is easy to remove fish bones with sharp chopsticks.

Head over to South Korea and chopsticks look a bit different. There, chopsticks are flat and typically made of metal. That’s because South Koreans love barbecue. The metal chopsticks won’t burn when diners are grilling their meat tableside.

While we sleep, research indicates, the brain replays the patterns of activity it experienced during waking hours, allowing us to enter what one psychologist calls a neural virtual reality. A vivid example of such replay can be seen in a video researchers made recently about sleep disorders. They taught a series of dance moves to a group of patients with conditions like sleepwalking, in which the sleeper engages in the kind of physical movement that does not normally occur during sleep. They then videotaped the subjects as they slept. Lying in bed, eyes closed, one female patient on the tape performs the dance moves she learned earlier.

This shows that while our bodies are at rest, our brains are drawing what’s important from the information and events we’ve recently encountered, then integrating that data into the vast store of what we already know. In a study, researchers at Harvard Medical School reported that college students who dreamed about a computer maze task they had learned showed a 10-fold improvement in their ability to find their way through the maze compared with participants who did not dream about the task.

One of the Harvard researchers, suggests that studying right before bedtime or taking a nap following a study session in the afternoon might increase the odds of dreaming about the material.

### Group 5

Back in 2011, a physicist at the University of Sydney went viral after he placed a grape in the microwave and filmed the fiery aftermath.

But why a grape turns into a fireball in a microwave?

The grape is less like an antenna and more like a trombone, though for microwaves instead of sound. When you play a trombone, you push vibrating air into it. The trombone will only sustain vibrations of a particular wavelength—the musical note you hear—depending on where you’ve positioned the slide. Only certain wavelengths, known as standing waves, fit perfectly inside the trombone. As vibrating air of various wavelengths enter the trombone, the standing waves add constructively, while other wavelengths cancel each other out. In other words, the trombone amplifies the standing waves and mutes all others.

The grape, incidentally, is the perfect size for amplifying the microwaves that your kitchen machine radiates. The appliance pushes microwaves into the two grape halves, where the waves bounce around and add constructively to focus the energy to a spot on the skin. Both grape halves happen to focus the energy to the same tiny point. That intense energy jostles the atoms and molecules at that spot, heating them up so much that they can no longer hold onto their electrons, which turns them into a plasma—and boom, fireball.

短文翻译

2011年，悉尼大学的一位物理学家（physicist）将一颗葡萄放入了微波炉（microwave），结果（aftermath）起火了（fiery），他将这个过程拍了下来，从而走红网络（go viral）。

但为什么葡萄在微波炉中会变成一个火球（fireball）呢？

这颗葡萄与其说像一个天线（antenna），不如说像一个长号（trombone），不过它处理的是微波而不是声音。当你吹奏长号时，你将振动（vibrate）的空气推入长号。长号只能维持特定波长（wavelength）的振动（vibration）——你听到的音符——取决于滑管（slide）的把位。只有特定波长的振动，即驻波（standing wave），才能完全地进入长号内部。当不同波长的空气振动进入长号时，驻波会有效（constructively）累积，而其他波长的振动则会相互抵消（cancel out）。换句话说，长号能放大驻波，并使所有其他波长失声（mute）。

顺便提一下（incidentally），葡萄的大小非常适合放大微波炉这一厨房用具辐射出（radiate）的微波。微波炉将微波推入两半葡萄中，微波在葡萄中反弹（bounce），有效地将能量集中到葡萄皮（skin）上的某个点。两半葡萄恰好将能量集中到同一个小点上。强烈的能量使该点上的原子（atom）和分子（molecule）相互碰撞（jostle），温度急剧升高，使它们无法再附着在电子（electron）上，从而变成了等离子体（plasma）——“轰（boom）”的一声，火球产生了。
