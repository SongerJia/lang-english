### Group 1

Why do people feel pain? The feeling of pain helps the brain protect the body. When a person stubs their toe, the immediate pain tells them that they need to watch where they’re walking. When they’re stung by a bee, pain tells them not to bother bees anymore.

Pain alerts the brain that a person may be sick. A painful throat could mean they caught a cold. Pain in the stomach might turn out to be a stomach bug or another issue. Without these pains, it would be much harder for people to tell when they’re sick.

But how exactly does pain work? When a person encounters something that causes damage to tissue, like a cut or burn, it’s noticed by nerve endings in the skin. These nerve endings then send a message along nerve fibres to the spinal cord. Once the spinal cord gets the message, it sends it to the brain.

Inside the brain, the thalamus sends out information about the pain to different parts of the brain. Once the brain has interpreted the pain and decided what to do about it, it sends that message back to the site of pain. That’s when the person reacts.

疼痛向大脑发出警告（alert）：一个人可能生病了。喉咙（throat）疼痛可能意味着感冒了。肚子疼痛可能是因为胃部感染（bug）或其他问题。如果没有这些疼痛，人们很难在自己生病时判断出来。

那么疼痛到底是如何起作用的呢？当一个人遇到对身体组织造成损伤的情况时，比如割伤或烧伤，皮肤上的神经末梢（nerve endings）会注意到。这些神经末梢然后沿着神经纤维向脊髓（spinal cord）发送信息。一旦脊髓接收到信息，就会将其发送到大脑。

### Group 2

When it comes to bones, babies have an interesting advantage over adults—they have around 100 more bones!

As a newborn, a baby’s skeleton is much more flexible and malleable compared to that of an adult. It consists of approximately 300 bones, while adults have only 206. This difference arises because some of these bones fuse together during growth and development.

Babies are born with numerous cartilaginous (软骨的) areas, also known as growth plates, between their bones. These growth plates allow for growth and development throughout infancy and childhood. As the baby grows older, these growth plates gradually harden and fuse, forming a single bone. Hence, the total bone count decreases as the infant grows into adulthood.

Bone fusion occurs as part of human development. At birth, the skeleton needs to be more flexible to accommodate the birthing process. Over time, the fusion of bones provides stability and strength necessary for upright movement. It is a natural and essential process that allows babies to transition into capable adults.

### Group 3

Teeth are a vital indicator of our overall health. We need them for biting and chewing, but our teeth also ensure our jaw develops properly and aid speech. Not looking after our teeth can cause tooth decay and gum disease, leading to the removal of the offending teeth. And although they may resemble bones, but are teeth considered bones?

Teeth and bones look similar and share some commonalities, including being the hardest substances in your body. But teeth aren’t actually bones. This misconception might arise from the fact that they both contain calcium. More than 99 percent of your body’s calcium can be found in your bones and teeth. Approximately 1 percent is found in your blood.

Bones are living tissue. They’re made up of the protein collagen and the mineral calcium phosphate. This enables bones to be strong but flexible. Teeth are not living tissue. They’re comprised of four different types of tissue: dentine, enamel, cementum, pulp.

Bones can repair and heal themselves, while teeth cannot. Teeth are more fragile in that respect, which is why it’s so important to practise good dental hygiene and see a dentist regularly.

### Group 4

You might think of your teeth as tools, like built-in knives and forks, but if they are mere tools, why do they feel pain? Wouldn’t it be better if they could just bite and chew under any conditions?

In spite of our dental discomfort, it turns out there’s a good reason our teeth are so sensitive. Tooth pain is a defence mechanism that ensures when a tooth is being damaged we’ll notice and do something about it.

If we eat something too hot or too cold, or if the tooth is worn down enough where the tissue underneath is exposed, all of those things cause pain, and then the pain causes the person not to use that tooth to try to protect it a little bit more.

Teeth have three layers, only one of which—the innermost layer of the tooth—can hurt. That innermost layer of the tooth is called the pulp and contains both blood vessels and nerves. Pain is the only sensation to which the nerves in the pulp respond. Whereas people with tooth sensitivity may complain, for example, of tooth pain triggered by heat or cold, the nerves in the pulp don’t sense temperature. Rather, they feel pain, which may be associated with, say, drinking a very cold milkshake.

短文翻译

研究人员汇总（pool）了23项研究的数据，发现社交孤立（isolation）或孤独感与冠心病（coronary heart disease）和中风（stroke）的风险增加有关。

这些研究涵盖了181 006名成年人的数据。在3至21年的随访期间，有4628起冠心病事件和3002起中风事件。其中3篇论文评估（measure）了孤独感，18篇研究了社交孤立，还有两篇同时包括了两者。

科学家们发现，孤独感和社交孤立使心脏病发作（heart attack）或死于心脏病的相对（relative）风险增加了29％，使中风风险增加了32％。男性和女性之间没有差异。

该研究的首席作者，英国约克大学的研究员妮科尔·K.瓦尔托塔说：“人们往往从政策（policy）角度出发，专注于针对（target）孤独（lonely）人群，以增强他们的社交联系。但我们的研究表明（demonstrate），如果这是一种风险因素，那么我们应该首先尽力预防这种风险因素的出现。”

作者承认，这是对观察性（observational）研究进行的综述，并未证实其因果关系（cause and effect）。
