### Group 1

Most of us know that junk food is unhealthy. We know that poor nutrition is related to heart problems, high blood pressure, and a host of other health ailments. You might even know that studies show that eating junk food has been linked to an increase in depression. But we can’t stop eating it. Why do our taste buds so frequently override our brains and tell us that eating that bag of potato chips, or that second snack cake, is worth the risk?

According to a food scientist, Witherly, when you eat tasty food, there are two factors that make the experience pleasurable. First, there is the sensation of eating the food. This includes what it tastes like (salty, sweet, umami, etc.), what it smells like, and how it feels in your mouth. The second factor is the actual macronutrient makeup of the food — the blend of proteins, fats, and carbohydrates that it contains. In the case of junk food, food manufacturers are looking for a perfect combination of salt, sugar, and fat that excites your brain and gets you coming back for more.

### Group 2

Caffeine can be part of a healthy diet for most people. Caffeine can be found naturally in the plants we use to make coffee, tea and chocolate. It’s also found in some plants used as flavourings.

Caffeine may also be added as an ingredient to foods and beverages. Many packaged foods, including beverages and dietary supplements containing caffeine, voluntarily provide information on the label as to how much caffeine they contain. However, too much may be dangerous for your health. Depending on factors such as body weight, medications you may take, and individual sensitivity, “too much” can vary from person to person.

For healthy adults, 400 milligrams a day—that’s about four or five cups of coffee — is an amount not generally associated with dangerous, negative effects.

Over-consuming caffeine can cause insomnia, anxiousness, a fast heart rate, an upset stomach, nausea, headaches and so on.

The risk of caffeine overdose increases as the concentration of caffeine in the product increases, meaning even small dosages of a highly concentrated product could lead to dangerous effects.

短文翻译

医疗专家们一直试图通过关注不同食物中的营养成分（nutrient），强调（emphasize）碳水化合物、脂肪、糖和卡路里的含量（content），以应对肥胖症的流行（epidemic），但他们取得的成效有限。

营销（marketing）专家认为，解决肥胖问题的真正方法（solution）是让食物看起来诱人，同时关注人们观看和品尝食物时的愉悦感。事实上，该团队的研究表明，将健康食品与令人愉悦的形象、体验和情感联系（associate）起来，会提高人们购买或食用这些食品的兴趣。他们声称（claim），比起提醒消费者某种食物对他们有好处，“这是一个更好的策略”。

研究人员引用（cite）了一项旨在扭转（reverse）胡萝卜销量下滑趋势的市场营销活动。该活动没有强调胡萝卜对健康有益的五个特点，而是强调它们的美丽和食用它们所带来的愉悦感。例如，广告侧重于胡萝卜鲜艳美丽的橙色和脆爽的（crisp）口感。该活动使产品销量激增10%以上。研究人员认为，其他公司也可以通过类似的广告，将他们的产品描绘成吸引人的（attractive）、能带来愉悦感的食物，从而提高健康食品的销量。
