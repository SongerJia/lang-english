### Group 1

Boredom has become trendy. Studies point to how boredom is good for creativity and innovation, as well as mental health. It is found that people are more creative following the completion of a tedious task. When people are bored, they have an increase in “associative thought”—the process of making new connections between ideas, which is linked to innovative thinking. These studies are impressive, but in reality, the benefits of boredom may be related to having time to clear your mind, be quiet, or daydream.

In our stimulation-rich world, it seems unrealistic that boredom could occur at all. Yet, there are valid reasons why boredom may make people feel so painful. As it turns out, boredom might signal the fact that you have a need that isn’t being met.

If your definition of boredom is being quiet, mindful, and reflective, keep it up. But if you’re struggling with real boredom and the emptiness it provokes, consider whether you might seek new connections and more significant challenges. These are the things that will genuinely relieve boredom and make you more effective in the process.

### Group 5

Have you ever noticed how frequently your mind returns to problems and situations that cause you pain, and insists on rehashing what’s wrong? It’s a strange phenomenon really, our addiction to thinking about problems. Even when we don’t want to think about what’s bothering us, still, we keep thinking about it. Why do we do this?

We return to painful situations because, at the root, we believe that every problem can be figured out with more thinking.

Simultaneously, we keep rehashing our problems because it feels like thinking about the situation is a way of empathizing with our pain. Going over the difficulty again and again is our attempt to offer ourselves compassion.

Furthermore, we keep returning to what hurts because if we let it go, stop thinking about it, then it’s as if we will be dishonouring how much it hurts. To stop thinking about our problems would be to behave as if our pain doesn’t matter. In essence, to abandon ourselves. In this way, our obsessive thinking is an attempt to award our suffering with importance and care.

Finally, we are addicted to thinking about our problems because we are identified with our suffering. Who we are is a tapestry of what we’ve lived through, endured, and survived. We derive our identity, in large part, from what we suffer.

短文翻译

你有没有注意到，你的思维会频繁地回到那些让你痛苦的问题和情况上，并且坚持（insist）重新思考（rehash）哪里出了问题？这真是一种奇怪的现象，我们沉迷（addiction）于思考问题。即使我们不想思考困扰（bother）我们的事情，我们仍然会不断地思考它。我们为什么要这样做呢？

我们回想痛苦的（painful）情况，从根本上来说（at the root），是因为我们相信每个问题都可以通过更多的思考来解决。

同时（simultaneously），我们不断重复思考我们的问题，因为感觉思考该问题是对我们的痛苦感同身受（empathize）的一种方式。一遍又一遍地重温（go over）困难是我们在对自己表达同情（compassion）。

此外，我们不断地回想伤害（hurt）我们的事情，因为如果我们放手，停止思考它，那么就好像我们不重视（dishonour）它带来的伤害。停止思考我们的问题就是表现得好像我们的痛苦无关紧要。本质上（in essence），这是对自己的放弃。因此，我们过度（obsessive）思考是试图给予我们的痛苦（suffering）以重视和关心。

最后，我们沉溺于（be addicted to）思考自己的问题，因为我们被等同于（identify ... with ...）我们的遭遇。我们是谁，是由我们所经历、忍受（endure）和艰难度过的事情共同绘就的画卷（tapestry）。我们的身份（identity）很大程度上（in large part）来自我们所遭受的苦难。
